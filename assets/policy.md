# ISUFST School Policy

## Academic Integrity Policy

ISUFST upholds the principles of honesty, integrity, and intellectual ownership in all academic work. Every student is expected to submit work that is entirely their own, unless explicit collaboration is authorized by the instructor. The university treats academic dishonesty as a serious offense that undermines the value of education and the reputation of its graduates.

### Forms of Academic Dishonesty

Academic dishonesty includes but is not limited to the following:

- **Plagiarism:** Submitting another person's work, ideas, or phrasing as one's own without proper attribution. This includes copying text from books, journals, websites, or other students' papers, as well as submitting AI-generated content as original student output without disclosure.
- **Cheating:** Using unauthorized notes, devices, or assistance during examinations or graded activities.
- **Fabrication:** Inventing or falsifying data, citations, laboratory results, or any other information required in academic outputs.
- **Collusion:** Unauthorized collaboration with another student on work that is intended to be completed independently.
- **Impersonation:** Having another person take an examination or complete graded work in one's place, or doing the same for another student.
- **Falsification of Records:** Altering official academic documents, ID cards, medical certificates, or any other official document submitted to the university.

All written outputs are subject to review for authenticity. The university may employ plagiarism detection tools and other verification methods.

### Consequences for Academic Dishonesty

Sanctions are applied progressively based on the severity and frequency of the offense:

- **First Offense:** Grade of 0.00 on the subject requirement involved, and a written notice sent to the parent or guardian. The incident is recorded in the student's file.
- **Second Offense:** Failing grade (5.00) for the entire subject in which the offense occurred, plus mandatory counseling by the Guidance and Counseling Office.
- **Third Offense or Grave Offense:** Suspension of up to one (1) semester, or expulsion from the university, depending on the gravity of the act, as determined by the Student Disciplinary Tribunal.

## Student Conduct and Disciplinary Procedures

ISUFST is committed to maintaining an orderly, safe, and conducive learning environment for all members of its community. All students are expected to conduct themselves in a manner consistent with Filipino values of respect, responsibility, and community.

The disciplinary framework is governed by the university's Student Handbook and is consistent with the Manual of Regulations for Private Higher Education (MORPHE), CHED Memorandum Orders, and applicable laws of the Republic of the Philippines.

### Classification of Offenses

**Minor Offenses** are handled by the subject instructor or the OSAS and include:
- Tardiness and unexcused absences exceeding the allowed limit
- Violations of the dress code or uniform policy
- Using mobile phones or other devices during class without permission
- Littering or failure to maintain cleanliness in classrooms or common areas
- Disrespectful behavior toward classmates

**Moderate Offenses** are referred to the Department Chair or College Dean and include:
- Bullying, harassment, or intimidation of fellow students
- Vandalism or minor damage to university property
- Repeated minor offenses despite prior warning
- Creating disturbances in the library, canteen, or other common areas
- Gambling within university premises

**Serious (Grave) Offenses** are referred to the Student Disciplinary Tribunal and include:
- Physical assault or fighting within or immediately outside university premises
- Possession, use, or sale of prohibited drugs or regulated substances
- Carrying of deadly weapons on campus
- Sexual harassment or gender-based violence
- Hazing or initiation rites prohibited under Republic Act No. 8049, as amended
- Willful destruction of university property or equipment
- Unauthorized posting of defamatory material against members of the university community
- Acts of insubordination or threats against faculty, staff, or university officials

### Disciplinary Procedures

Upon a formal complaint or report, the following process is observed:

1. **Fact-Finding:** The OSAS or the Student Disciplinary Tribunal initiates an inquiry to determine the facts of the case.
2. **Notice to the Student:** The student receives a written notice stating the nature of the offense and a schedule for a formal hearing.
3. **Hearing:** The student is given the opportunity to present their side. Parents or guardians may be required to attend. The student may be accompanied by a fellow student representative but not by a legal counsel during the hearing, unless the case has been elevated to formal legal proceedings.
4. **Decision:** The Disciplinary Tribunal issues a written decision within fifteen (15) school days from the conclusion of the hearing.
5. **Notification:** The decision is communicated in writing to the student and their parent or guardian.

Sanctions may include written reprimand, community service within the university, suspension, or expulsion, depending on the nature and gravity of the offense.

## Anti-Hazing Policy

In strict compliance with Republic Act No. 8049 (Anti-Hazing Act of 1995), as amended by Republic Act No. 11053 (Anti-Hazing Act of 2018), ISUFST absolutely prohibits hazing in all forms within and outside the campus.

Hazing is defined as any act that results in physical or psychological suffering, harm, or injury inflicted on a recruit, neophyte, or applicant of any organization as a prerequisite for admission or as a condition for continued membership. This prohibition applies to all student organizations, fraternities, sororities, and other groups registered with ISUFST.

Any student found to have participated in or organized hazing activities will be subject to the full penalties provided under RA 11053, including expulsion from the university. The organization responsible will be permanently banned from operating within the university. The university will also cooperate fully with law enforcement authorities in any criminal investigation arising from hazing incidents.

## Anti-Sexual Harassment and Safe Spaces Policy

ISUFST is committed to maintaining a safe, respectful, and inclusive environment free from sexual harassment and gender-based violence. The university enforces Republic Act No. 7877 (Anti-Sexual Harassment Act of 1995) and Republic Act No. 11313 (Safe Spaces Act), also known as the "Bawal Bastos" Law.

### Prohibited Acts

The following acts are prohibited within the university campus and in university-related activities, whether in person or online:
- Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or any conduct of a sexual nature that creates a hostile or offensive environment
- Catcalling, wolf-whistling, misogynistic remarks, or any form of gender-based street harassment
- Sharing of sexual content without consent, including through electronic means
- Discriminatory remarks based on gender identity or sexual orientation
- Any physical act of a sexual nature without consent

### How to Report

Complaints may be filed with the Committee on Decorum and Investigation (CODI) or the Gender and Development (GAD) Focal Point Office. Complainants may file a written complaint addressed to the CODI Chairperson. All complaints are treated with strict confidentiality. Retaliation against a complainant is itself a disciplinary offense.

## Use of School Property and Facilities

Students are entrusted with the responsible use and care of all university property, including but not limited to: classrooms, laboratory equipment, library materials, sports and recreational facilities, computer equipment, canteen, and dormitory facilities (for boarders).

### Responsibility and Accountability

- Students who damage school property through negligence or deliberate action are financially liable for the full replacement or repair cost as assessed by the university.
- Payment for damages must be settled at the Cashier's Office within thirty (30) days of the assessment. Failure to do so will result in a hold on the student's academic records and clearances.
- Students must report any observed damage to university property to the nearest faculty member, staff, or security guard immediately.

### Computer Laboratories and Internet Use

The use of ISUFST's computer laboratories and internet connection is governed by the Acceptable Use Policy, which all students must acknowledge during enrollment. Key provisions include:
- School computers are for academic use only.
- Accessing pornographic, gambling, or other inappropriate websites is strictly prohibited.
- Downloading or installing unauthorized software is not allowed.
- Students are prohibited from attempting to bypass network security measures.
- Personal use of school computers for commercial transactions (online selling, cryptocurrency activities, etc.) is not permitted.

Violations of the Acceptable Use Policy are subject to disciplinary action, restriction from computer laboratory access, and reporting to law enforcement if applicable laws are violated.

## Social Media and Online Conduct Policy

Students of ISUFST are expected to represent the university with dignity and respect in all online spaces. The university's policies on conduct apply equally to actions taken in cyberspace, including outside of school hours, if such actions have an adverse impact on the university community.

### Prohibited Online Conduct

The following are prohibited under this policy:
- Cyberbullying or online harassment of fellow students, faculty, staff, or university officials
- Posting or sharing of content that is defamatory, threatening, sexually explicit, or discriminatory
- Impersonating ISUFST officials, faculty, or staff in any online platform
- Sharing of confidential information pertaining to the university, its students, or its operations without authorization
- Creating or joining online groups or pages that are used to organize activities that violate university policies or Philippine law
- Spreading misinformation or fake news that damages the reputation of the university or any member of its community

### Consequences

Students found to have violated this policy may be subject to disciplinary action ranging from a written reprimand to suspension or expulsion, depending on the nature of the offense. The university will cooperate with the National Privacy Commission (NPC) and law enforcement agencies, particularly in cases involving violations of Republic Act No. 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012).

## Data Privacy Policy

ISUFST collects and processes student personal data in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173, also known as the Data Privacy Act of 2012, and its implementing rules and regulations. The university's Data Protection Officer (DPO) oversees compliance with this law.

### Data Collected

The university may collect the following types of personal information from students:
- Personal identifiers (full name, date of birth, place of birth, home address, contact numbers)
- Family background information (names of parents or guardians, their occupation and address)
- Academic records (grades, enrollment status, examination results)
- Health and medical records
- Financial information (for scholarship and financial assistance processing)
- Behavioral and disciplinary records

### Purpose of Collection

Personal data is collected solely for legitimate educational and administrative purposes, including:
- Enrollment processing and academic records management
- Issuance of official documents (transcripts, certificates, diplomas)
- Scholarship and financial assistance processing
- Student welfare and guidance services
- Alumni tracking and engagement

### Disclosure to Third Parties

Student records will not be disclosed to third parties without the written consent of the student (or their parent or guardian if the student is a minor), except:
- When required by a lawful order from a court or competent government authority
- When disclosure is required by CHED, UNIFAST, or other oversight agencies for accreditation, audit, or regulatory compliance
- When disclosure is necessary to protect the life, health, or safety of the student or others

### Rights of Data Subjects

Students and their parents or guardians (for minor students) have the following rights under the Data Privacy Act:
- **Right to be informed:** To know what data is being collected and why.
- **Right to access:** To request a copy of their personal data held by the university.
- **Right to correct:** To request correction of inaccurate or outdated information.
- **Right to erasure or blocking:** To request deletion of personal data that is no longer necessary, in cases permitted by law.
- **Right to object:** To object to the processing of personal data for certain purposes.
- **Right to file a complaint:** To file a complaint with the National Privacy Commission if data privacy rights are violated.

Requests related to data privacy may be directed to the university's Data Protection Officer at the Office of the Registrar.

## Appeals and Grievance Procedures

Students and their parents or guardians have the right to appeal academic and disciplinary decisions through the university's formal grievance mechanism.

### Grounds for Appeal

An appeal may be filed on the following grounds:
- Procedural irregularities in the disciplinary proceedings
- New evidence that was not available at the time of the original decision
- Grave abuse of discretion or manifest bias on the part of the deciding authority
- Disproportionate or unjust penalty in relation to the offense committed

### How to File an Appeal

1. The student or parent must submit a written appeal letter to the Office of the Campus Director (or College Dean for academic matters) within ten (10) school days from receipt of the original decision.
2. The appeal letter must clearly state the grounds for the appeal and be accompanied by supporting documents.
3. An Appeals Review Committee, composed of a representative of the OSAS, the College Dean (or designee), a faculty representative, and an SSG representative, will evaluate the appeal.
4. The Committee will issue a written resolution within fifteen (15) school days from the filing of the appeal.

### Further Escalation

If the student remains unsatisfied with the resolution of the Appeals Review Committee, the matter may be elevated to the Office of the University President within ten (10) school days of receiving the Committee's resolution. The President's decision is final and executory within the university. If the student believes their rights under Philippine law have been violated, they may bring the matter to CHED, the Commission on Human Rights, or other appropriate government agencies.

All appeal proceedings are strictly confidential and conducted in a manner consistent with the rights of all parties involved.

## Environmental and Campus Cleanliness Policy

In accordance with Republic Act No. 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act), ISUFST enforces strict waste segregation and environmental protection measures within the campus.

Students are expected to:
- Dispose of waste only in designated segregated bins (biodegradable, non-biodegradable, recyclable, and special waste).
- Refrain from littering in any part of the campus, including hallways, grounds, canteen areas, and sports facilities.
- Participate in campus clean-up drives and environmental programs organized by the university or the SSG.
- Avoid single-use plastics in line with applicable local ordinances and national environmental regulations.

Violations of this policy are subject to community service penalties and disciplinary action for repeated offenders.
